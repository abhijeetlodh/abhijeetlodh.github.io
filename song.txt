### 1. Document: PPW Parser & Protocol Data Handling Design

**1.1 Business Objective & Extraction Workflow**
The Flexible Dosing Forecast Tool answers a specific business question for cycle-based studies: "Given a protocol's visit cycles and dosing rules, how much drug (and packaging) will we need, and by when?" It relies on deterministic calculation rather than guessing missing information. The extraction and computation workflow follows 10 defined steps:

* **Step 1 - Receive Study Information:** The tool ingests building blocks including visit schedules, kits, dispensing rules, treatment groups, dosing plans, cycle definitions, and optional business assumptions (e.g., weight/age/BSA distributions, country-specific rules).
* **Step 2 - Mode Selection:** Operations are routed through three paths: Quick Check (validation), Standard Forecast (calculation), or Full Report (rehydration). If a standard forecast was already run, the system uses a Rehydration Path utilizing a saved `run_id` to instantly generate the report without recomputing.


* **Step 3 - Essentials Validation:** The system confirms internal consistency (visits, kits, dispensing sets, treatment groups) and verifies cycle essentials (structure, duration, schedule). If critical data is missing, execution stops.
* **Step 4 - Treatment Timeline Generation:** A full schedule of dosing events is laid out across the study, mapping each cycle and visit over time based on the forecast horizon.
* **Step 5 - Dose Determination:** The tool identifies the correct dose and kit for every visit on the timeline based on priority rules (overrides first, then selected options), accounting for cycle-aware dose changes.
* **Step 6 - Patient Variation Accounting:** If dosing relies on characteristics (weight, age, BSA, titration), planned patients are split across categories. This is skipped if not applicable.
* **Step 7 - Country-Specific Rules:** The tool checks supply rules, substituting approved kit alternatives where required by specific countries to reflect real-world constraints.
* **Step 8 - Demand Calculation:** Patient-driven demand (patients × units per dose × cycle repetitions + safety buffer) is calculated alongside operational demand (site/depot seeding).
* **Step 9 - Results Production:** Output tables are generated, presenting the detailed dispensing matrix and summaries by forecast, visit, treatment group, cycle, and assumption.
* **Step 10 - Save, Report, and Export:** The forecast is stamped with a unique `run_id`, persisting in `AI_SP_PAYLOAD_HANDOFF` for future rehydration. It can export to a formatted Excel workbook based on environment configurations.



**1.2 Table Design, Data Mapping, and Sourcing**
The extracted payload is disaggregated into specific tables. Below is the detailed mapping of what data goes into each table and its source logic (IMPALA2 vs. PPW Document):

* **`AI_SP_PPW_VISITS`**

* **`AI_SP_PPW_CYCLES`**
* **Data Stored:** `study_number`, `cycle_id`, `cycle_label`, `planned_cycle_count`, `cycle_duration_days`, `phase`.
* **Extraction Source:** Cycle boundaries and numeric sequences are constructed from IMPALA2 phases, but qualitative narratives and custom boundaries are PPW/Protocol Document driven.


* **`AI_SP_PPW_CYCLE_VISITS`**
* **Data Stored:** `study_number`, `cycle_id`, `phase`, `visit_id`, `visit_sequence`, `cycle_day`, `visit_window_start_day`, `visit_window_end_day`.
* **Extraction Source:** Mapped by joining IMPALA2 phases and visits. Custom normalized IDs (e.g., `TNC_C1_4_D1`) are derived from the PPW document.

* **`AI_SP_PPW_DISPENSING_RULES`**
* **Data Stored:** `RULE_SEQUENCE`, `CONJUNCTION`, `DEPENDENCY`.

* **Extraction Source:** Highly dependent on PPW. Clinical dose values, multi-choice flexible/fixed dosing logic, and specific units (mg/kg) are strictly PPW or Protocol Document driven.

 # AI Supply Plan — PPW Extraction and Snowflake Data Model

 ## 1\. Purpose

 The purpose of this solution is to extract protocol information directly from the Protocol Parameter Worksheet (PPW) and make it available to the AI Supply Plan forecasting process without depending on the availability or latency of the legacy IMPALA2 database process.

 The solution supports the following business question:

 > **Given a protocol's visit cycles and dosing rules, how much drug and packaging will be required, and by when?**

 The solution is specifically designed for **cycle-based flexible-dosing studies**, where treatment is repeated across defined cycles and visits, for example:

 - Cycle 1 / Day 1
- Cycle 1 / Day 15
- Cycle 2 / Day 1
- Cycle 2 / Day 15
- etc.

 The design deliberately avoids inventing missing protocol information. If a required value cannot be established from the PPW/protocol or an approved source mapping, the process flags the missing information instead of generating an assumed value.

---

 # 2\. Architectural Objective

 The existing conversational-agent implementation is not suitable for the required forecasting SLA because PDF extraction through the interactive agent can consume several minutes.

 The new architecture moves PPW extraction into a **Dataiku DSS backend process**.

 The target processing flow is:

```
PPW PDF
   ↓
Dataiku Managed Folder
   ↓
Fast PDF Text/Table Extraction
   ↓
LLM Structured Extraction
   ↓
Validation & Normalization
   ↓
Split Structured Payload
   ↓
5 AI_SP_PPW_* Logical Tables
   ↓
S3 / Dataiku Staging
   ↓
Snowflake
   ↓
Forecast Engine
   ↓
Drug + Packaging Demand
   ↓
Report / Excel Export
```

 The architecture uses the five target tables to avoid creating one large denormalized PPW payload table.

 # 3\. PPW Extraction Process

 ## 3.1 Input

 The input is a PPW PDF uploaded into the Dataiku Managed Folder:

```
PPW_DOCUMENTS
```

 The document is associated with a `study_code` / `study_number`, for example:

```
C6461001
C4771012
```

 The study code is the primary processing identifier.

---

 ## 3.2 File Detection

 The Dataiku scenario monitors the `PPW_DOCUMENTS` managed folder.

 When a file is detected:

 1. Identify the study code.
2. Check whether the file already exists.
3. Compare file metadata/hash.
4. If the file is an exact duplicate, skip processing.
5. If the file is new, process it.
6. If the same study has a modified PPW, reprocess it.
7. Compare the newly extracted data with existing Snowflake data.
8. If material differences are detected, flag the study for manual review rather than silently replacing data.

 # 4\. Extraction Architecture

 ## 4.1 Fast PDF Extraction

 The PDF extraction process uses a lightweight PDF parser such as:

 - PyMuPDF
- pdfplumber

 The extraction process focuses on relevant PPW content, particularly sections containing terms such as:

```
Visit
Cycle
Treatment
Dispense
Dosing
Phase
Kit
Cohort
Arm
```

 The extraction process should minimize unnecessary PDF processing to meet the performance objective.

 ### Important design principle

 The optimization should not compromise data completeness.

 If required information is located beyond the initial page range, the extraction strategy must be capable of locating the relevant pages rather than assuming that the first 35–40 pages always contain every required field.

 # 5\. LLM Extraction

 The extracted PPW text is sent to the Dataiku LLM Mesh.

 The LLM produces a structured JSON payload containing the logical PPW domains.

 Conceptually:

```
{
  "study": {},
  "visits": [],
  "cycles": [],
  "cycle_visits": [],
  "treatment_groups": [],
  "dosing_plan": []
}
```

 The LLM is instructed to:

 - extract only information present in the source;
- preserve protocol terminology;
- never invent missing values;
- distinguish PPW-driven information from IMPALA2-derived information;
- return null/unknown when the source does not provide a value;
- preserve study-level lineage;
- produce data that can be normalized into the five target tables.

---

 # 6\. Data Validation

 Before data is written to Snowflake, the extracted payload is validated.

 The validation layer checks:

 ### Study-level consistency

 - Study number exists.
- Study number matches the document.
- Required PPW sections are available.

 ### Visit consistency

 - Visits have valid identifiers.
- Visit sequence is available.
- Visit/cycle relationships are valid.
- Visit windows are internally consistent.

 ### Cycle consistency

 - Cycle definitions exist where required.
- Cycle start/end boundaries can be established.
- No arbitrary cycle duration is assumed.

 ### Treatment-group consistency

 - Treatment groups are identified.
- Treatment-group/visit relationships are valid.
- Planned subjects are available where required.

 ### Dosing consistency

 - Dose information is traceable to PPW/protocol or approved legacy lineage.
- Dosing rules are not inferred from kit descriptions alone.
- Country-specific rules are not fabricated.
- Missing clinical dosing attributes are explicitly flagged.

 These do **not necessarily become separate physical Snowflake tables** under the five-table target architecture.

 Instead, their forecasting-relevant attributes are normalized into:

```
AI_SP_PPW_DOSING_PLAN
```

 This is important to document because otherwise a reviewer may ask:

 > "Where did the kit information go if there is no AI\_SP\_PPW\_KITS table?"

 The answer is:

 > Kit and dispensing-set attributes are part of the dosing/dispensing business domain and are represented within `AI_SP_PPW_DOSING_PLAN` in the current five-table physical model.

 If the business later requires independent kit-level reporting or inventory management, a separate `AI_SP_PPW_KITS` table can be introduced as a future physical-model extension.

 # 14\. Important Source-Lineage Principle

 Not every field should be derived from IMPALA2.

 The mapping should explicitly classify fields into three categories.

 ### Category A — Direct technical lineage

 Example:

```
STUDY_NUMBER
VISIT_SEQUENCE
DESCRIPTION
PHASE
```

 These can be directly mapped from known IMPALA2 source fields.

 ### Category B — Deterministic transformation

 Example:

```
VISIT_ID
CYCLE_LABEL
CYCLE_DAY
NUMBER_OF_DISPENSING_VISITS
```

 These can be constructed using documented transformation logic.

 ### Category C — PPW / protocol-driven

 Examples:

```
DOSE_VALUE
DOSE_UNIT
DOSE_BASIS
TREATMENT_GROUP_DESCRIPTION
COUNTRY_VIAL_STRENGTH_RULES
KIT_TYPE
DOSE_DESCRIPTION
```

 These must come from the PPW/protocol or a governed mapping.

 **The system must not infer these values from unrelated technical fields.**

 # 15\. Forecast Processing

 Once the five Snowflake tables are populated, the downstream forecast process uses them in the following sequence.

 ## Step 1 — Validate

 Validate:

```
VISITS
CYCLES
CYCLE_VISITS
TREATMENT_GROUPS
DOSING_PLAN
```

 and verify that cross-references are valid.

---

 ## Step 2 — Build Treatment Timeline

 Combine:

```
CYCLES
+
CYCLE_VISITS
+
TREATMENT_GROUPS
```

 to create the treatment timeline.

---

 ## Step 3 — Resolve Dose

 For every treatment event:

```
Treatment Group
        +
Cycle
        +
Visit
        ↓
DOSING_PLAN
        ↓
Dose + Kit + Dispensing Rule
```

 The dose-selection hierarchy must respect explicit overrides and governed PPW rules.

---

 ## Step 4 — Patient Variation

 Where relevant, patient characteristics are applied:

```
Weight
Age
BSA
Titration
Population
```

 If the dosing model requires these assumptions but they are not available, the process flags the missing information.

---

 ## Step 5 — Country Rules

 Country-specific supply constraints are applied.

 For example:

```
Country
   ↓
Allowed presentation
   ↓
Allowed vial strength
   ↓
Approved kit
```

 The system must not infer country applicability merely from a kit identifier.

---

 ## Step 6 — Demand Calculation

 The calculation conceptually becomes:

```
Patients
×
Dose / Units per Dose
×
Frequency
×
Cycle / Visit Repetition
+
Safety Buffer
+
Operational Demand
=
Forecast Demand
```

 Operational demand such as site/depot seeding is kept separate from patient-driven demand.

---

 # 16\. Outputs

 The forecasting process produces:

 - detailed dispensing matrix;
- forecast summary;
- visit-level summary;
- treatment-group summary;
- cycle-level summary;
- assumption summary;
- drug demand;
- packaging demand.

 Each result is associated with a unique:

```
run_id
```

 The result can then be:

 - saved;
- reopened;
- reported;
- exported to Excel.

 # 17\. Recalculation / Report Reuse

 A useful architectural capability from the existing client pattern should also be documented.

 If a standard forecast has already been calculated and saved, a subsequent full-report request should be able to retrieve the saved result using `run_id` instead of recalculating the forecast.

 Conceptually:

```
run_id
  ↓
Saved Forecast
  ↓
Rehydrate
  ↓
Generate Report
```

 This ensures:

 1. faster report generation;
2. identical numbers between the original forecast and regenerated report;
3. no unnecessary recalculation.

 # 22\. Design Principles

 The final design should explicitly state these principles.

 ### 1\. No single massive PPW table

 The payload is split into five business-oriented relational domains.

 ### 2\. PPW is the source of truth for newly available protocol information

 The system does not wait for late-arriving database records when the PPW already contains the information.

 ### 3\. IMPALA2 is used for governed technical lineage where appropriate

 Existing IMPALA2 mappings are retained where the source fields are deterministic and validated.

 ### 4\. PPW-driven fields remain PPW-driven

 Clinical information such as dosing narrative, dose basis, country-specific applicability and treatment interpretation must not be inferred from technical database identifiers.

 ### 5\. Missing information is not guessed

 If an essential value is unavailable:

```
Missing → Validation Error / Manual Resolution
```

 not:

```
Missing → Arbitrary Default
```

 ### 6\. Existing study data is protected

 A modified PPW does not silently overwrite an existing study.

 The process detects differences and supports controlled resolution.

 ### 7\. Snowflake accumulation is append/upsert based

 New study data is added to the existing dataset without destructive table replacement.

 ### 8\. Forecast calculations remain traceable

 Every forecast value should be traceable through:

```
run_id
 ↓
Treatment Group
 ↓
Cycle
 ↓
Visit
 ↓
Dosing Rule
 ↓
Kit / Presentation
 ↓
Demand
```

### Document: Dataiku DSS Extraction Architecture & Table Design

**1. Architectural Overview & Workflow**
To bypass the 3–5 minute latency of the legacy conversational agent, the PPW extraction has been refactored into a fully automated backend pipeline within Dataiku DSS.

* **The Trigger (DSS Scenario):** A Dataiku Scenario continuously monitors the `PPW_DOCUMENTS` folder. When a unique study document is uploaded, the scenario automatically triggers the pipeline.
* **The Engine (Python Recipe):** The core extraction logic runs inside a Dataiku Python Recipe. It takes the `study_number` as an input and processes the document.
* **The Output (S3 to Snowflake):** To avoid creating one monolithic dataset, the Python recipe splits the extracted JSON payload into 5 distinct tables. These are temporarily staged in DSS S3 Buckets before being seamlessly appended (Upsert) into the 5 target Snowflake tables using the strict `AI_SP_PPW_<TABLE_NAME>` naming convention.

**2. How the Extraction Works (Logic & Mapping)**
The Python Recipe uses a hybrid extraction approach, combining legacy database lookups with LLM-driven document parsing:

* **Native Mapping (Impala2):** Standardized fields (like base treatment codes A, B, C, D, and generic visit schedules) are mapped directly from existing `IMPALA2_NRT` tables.
* **LLM/Document Driven:** Complex, study-specific variables that do not exist in the database (e.g., composite treatment groups like `A_NAB`, specific qualitative cycle boundaries, clinical dosing rules, and country-specific vial logic) are extracted directly from the PPW document using the LLM parser.
* **Data Merging:** The recipe joins the PPW-derived data with the IMPALA2 native data using `STUDY_NUMBER` and `PHASE` as primary keys, formatting them to match the target Snowflake schema.

**3. Target Snowflake Tables & Data Dictionary**
Below is the explicit breakdown of what data goes into each of the 5 Snowflake tables:

* **`AI_SP_PPW_VISITS`**
* **Data Stored:** `STUDY_NUMBER`, `PHASE`, `VISIT_ID`, `VISIT_SEQUENCE`, `DESCRIPTION`, `CYCLE_LABEL`, `CYCLE_DAY`, `VISIT_WINDOW_START_DAY`, `VISIT_WINDOW_END_DAY`, `NUMBER_OF_DISPENSING_VISITS`.
* **Extraction Details:** Mapped directly from `IMPALA2_NRT_VISIT_SCHEDULE` and `IMPALA2_NRT_STUDY_PHASES`. `VISIT_LABEL` is parsed using regex to extract numeric values. `NUMBER_OF_DISPENSING_VISITS` is calculated using a `COUNT_IF` grouping by study code and visit number.


* **`AI_SP_PPW_CYCLES`**
* **Data Stored:** `STUDY_NUMBER`, `CYCLE_ID`, `CYCLE_LABEL`, `PLANNED_CYCLE_COUNT`, `CYCLE_DURATION_DAYS`, `PHASE`.
* **Extraction Details:** Numeric boundaries are calculated from Impala2 phase data (`STP_CYCLE_STARTING_NUM + STP_MAX_REPEATING_NUM`). The qualitative cycle descriptions and planned cycle counts are strictly PPW Document-driven.


* **`AI_SP_PPW_CYCLE_VISITS`**
* **Data Stored:** `STUDY_NUMBER`, `CYCLE_ID`, `PHASE`, `VISIT_ID`, `VISIT_SEQUENCE`, `CYCLE_DAY`, `VISIT_WINDOW_START_DAY`, `VISIT_WINDOW_END_DAY`.
* **Extraction Details:** This acts as a junction table. It constructs the source visit IDs by aggregating configured visit orders, joining Phase data with Visit data. Normalized IDs (e.g., `TNC_C1_4_D1`) are derived from the PPW document.


* **`AI_SP_PPW_TREATMENT_GROUPS`**
* **Data Stored:** `STUDY_NUMBER`, `TREATMENT_GROUP_CODE`, `TREATMENT_GROUP_DESCRIPTION`, `COHORT_NUMBER`.
* **Extraction Details:** Native values (e.g., A, B, C, D) map directly from `IMPALA2_NRT_TREATMENT_GROUPS`. Composite values (e.g., `A_NAB`, `B_PAC`) and cohort logic are exclusively PPW Document-driven.


* **`AI_SP_PPW_DISPENSING_RULES`** (Encompasses Dosing Plan & Rules)
* **Data Stored:** `RULE_SEQUENCE`, `CONJUNCTION`, `DEPENDENCY`, `DOSING_MODE`, `DOSE_VALUE`, `DOSE_UNIT`, `DOSE_BASIS`.
* **Extraction Details:** `RULE_SEQUENCE`, `CONJUNCTION`, and `DEPENDENCY` map directly from `IMPALA2_NRT_DISPENSING_RULES`. Clinical dose values, multi-choice dosing modes (flexible vs. fixed), and specific units (mg vs. mg/kg) are strictly extracted from the PPW document.

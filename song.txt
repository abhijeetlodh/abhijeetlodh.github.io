PART 1 — BUSINESS CONTEXT

1.1 Initiative Name

Enterprise Developer Onboarding Standardization and Automation Framework

Alternative working name:

Developer Onboarding Hub

1.2 Business Problem

Developer onboarding across enterprise engineering teams is inconsistent, fragmented, manually coordinated, and dependent on tribal knowledge.

When a new developer joins an engineering team, they may encounter challenges involving:

- Understanding team responsibilities
- Understanding business applications
- Understanding enterprise architecture
- Identifying required systems
- Understanding databases
- Understanding development and testing environments
- Identifying appropriate access permissions
- Raising access requests
- Finding correct approval owners
- Understanding internal application dependencies
- Configuring developer workstations
- Setting up repositories
- Understanding CI/CD pipelines
- Accessing internal documentation
- Completing mandatory training
- Understanding code review standards
- Understanding logging and exception handling
- Understanding database connectivity
- Learning team-specific workflows
- Identifying support contacts
- Understanding production monitoring
- Learning deployment procedures
- Identifying escalation channels
- Completing initial development assignments

Existing documentation may be distributed across Confluence, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint, repositories, internal portals, and individual team members.

Different engineering teams may follow different onboarding procedures.

A single universal checklist may therefore be insufficient.

The proposed framework must balance standardization with team-specific flexibility.

1.3 Business Objectives

Design a framework that:

1. Standardizes common onboarding activities.
2. Supports team-specific onboarding requirements.
3. Reduces unnecessary manual coordination.
4. Improves developer productivity.
5. Improves access-request visibility.
6. Reduces repeated onboarding questions.
7. Makes internal systems easier to understand.
8. Improves documentation discoverability.
9. Defines clear ownership and accountability.
10. Supports continuous documentation maintenance.
11. Improves consistency across engineering teams.
12. Reuses existing enterprise tools.
13. Supports measurable onboarding progress.
14. Enables future automation where justified.
15. Provides a foundation for potential AI-assisted onboarding.

1.4 Initial Delivery Principle

Discovery and planning must precede implementation.

The initial assignment is NOT to build a complete onboarding application.

The first deliverable is a validated plan explaining:

- What exists today
- What problems exist
- What should be standardized
- What should remain team-specific
- What can be reused
- What may require development
- Which stakeholders are involved
- Which dependencies exist
- How implementation should be divided
- What a pilot should contain
- How success should be measured
